Money in, value out

Revenue taken in a month against voucher value redeemed in that same month. Both are dollars, on one axis — a month where redeemed sits well above revenue is a month the gym worked off earlier sales. Revenue counts sale and promo vouchers only — money actually taken. Redeemed value covers every class, including account credits being drawn down, so a credit redemption here is a real visit, not revenue the gym failed to collect.

How fast a cohort comes back

Of the value sold in each month, how much had been redeemed by 1, 3, 6 and 12 months. The bands are cumulative, so they are drawn side by side, never stacked.

A band is only drawn once the cohort is old enough to have one. A voucher sold last month cannot yet have a 12-month figure, so no 12-month bar is drawn for it — that gap is the honest answer, not a zero. Cohort months marked * are still younger than 12 months and therefore incomplete.

How to read the earlier years. Only a handful of the earliest redemptions were reconstructed rather than logged directly: 9 vouchers totalling $344.90, all redeemed before 2022, when redemption logging began. Every redemption from 2022 onwards is an exact logged transaction. Those 9 reconstructed redemptions are dated at the voucher's last redemption, so the month-to-month shape of the earliest years is approximate even though totals reconcile exactly. A handful of 2020–21 vouchers also carry no recorded value. All months are Perth months (Australia/Perth) and all money is in AUD.
